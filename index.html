What Is Linux?

Linux is a free and open-source family of operating systems that is resilient and flexible. The system's source code is accessible to everyone for anyone to look at and change, making it cool that anyone can see how the system works. People from all across the world are urged to work together and keep developing Linux due to its openness.

Since the beginning, Linux has grown into dependable and safe distros that are used in an array of gadgets, including PCs, phones, and huge supercomputers. Linux is cost-effective because it's free and lightweight, helping older machines stay functional longer.

Distros

You might be confused from what I meant by distros...

A Linux distribution, often shortened to “distro,” is a packaged version of Linux that comes with the Linux kernel plus a collection of software and utilities that make the OS functional and user-friendly.

Some distros are optimized for business, offering tools for productivity and enterprise management, while others focus on multimedia, bundling advanced audio and video editing software. Distributions for software developers come preloaded with compilers, programming tools, and integrated development environments (IDEs).

The 10 most common and used distros are:
