Distros

You might be confused from what I meant by distros...

A Linux distribution, often shortened to “distro,” is a packaged version of Linux that comes with the Linux kernel plus a collection of software and utilities that make the OS functional and user-friendly.

Some distros are optimized for business, offering tools for productivity and enterprise management, while others focus on multimedia, bundling advanced audio and video editing software. Distributions for software developers come preloaded with compilers, programming tools, and integrated development environments (IDEs).

The 10 most common and used distros are:

  1. Linux Mintbeginner's best friend
  2. CachyOSbuilt for speed (what I run)
  3. OpenSuse Leaprock solid & stable
  4. Elementary OSlooks gorgeous
  5. Ubuntuthe household name
  6. Fedora OSbleeding edge, done right
  7. Zorinwindows refugees start here
  8. Debianthe OG stability king
  9. Pop_OS!gamer & dev favorite
  10. MX Linuxruns on literally anything
💡 Tip: If you're new to Linux, I recommend using the distros listed here as they have a huge and active community and can easily help you out when you're stuck! (small update from me: I swapped Manjaro out of this list — it used to be a common beginner rec, but its update-delay setup on top of being rolling-release has caused enough real problems that I can't recommend it anymore. CachyOS is what I actually run daily now, and it's been solid.)
